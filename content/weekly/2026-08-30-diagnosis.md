Classic Golgari Yawgmoth had a thin week in published results, but the results that did appear reinforce two branches that have been developing for several weeks. **NickBolas90** went 5-0 in the August 26 MTGO League with the lands/Pawpatch build and followed it with a 4-3 finish in the 99-player August 27 Modern Challenge. In paper, **Adam Kropidłowski** finished second in a 15-player event on August 25 with the increasingly familiar 3-[[Yawgmoth, Thran Physician]], 2-[[Chord of Calling]], 1-[[Dredger's Insight]] configuration.

The broad numbers remain solid: MTGDecks currently reports a 54% 30-day win rate for Golgari Yawgmoth. The matchup spread is more important than the headline number. Izzet Prowess, Eldrazi Bloodchief Combo, Amulet Titan, Esper Blink, and especially Living End remain the problem matchups, while the recent Izzet Metalcraft results have improved substantially.

There is **no genuinely new Yawgmoth card this week**. [[Pawpatch Formation]], which first showed up in NickBolas90's sideboard last week, appeared again in both of Nick's published lists. That repetition upgrades it from a one-week experiment to a credible flexible sideboard option.

## Notes on the lists

**Adam Kropidłowski** gives the Chord branch another paper result. The main deck is 3 [[Yawgmoth, Thran Physician]], 3 [[Walking Ballista]], 4 [[Agatha's Soul Cauldron]], 3 [[Green Sun's Zenith]], 3 [[Malevolent Rumble]], 2 [[Chord of Calling]], 1 [[Dredger's Insight]], 1 [[Grist, the Hunger Tide]], and singleton utility creatures including [[Endurance]], [[Formidable Speaker]], [[Ouroboroid]], [[Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER]], and [[Virulent Emissary]]. The list also plays 2 [[Orcish Bowmasters]].

Adam's sideboard is especially relevant to the current field: 2 [[Ashiok, Dream Render]], 3 [[Fatal Push]], 2 [[Force of Vigor]], 3 [[Thoughtseize]], and one each of [[Culling Ritual]], Endurance, [[Outland Liberator]], [[Soulless Jailer]], and [[Vexing Bauble]]. The two Ashioks are a useful signal with Amulet Titan and graveyard/search decks remaining relevant.

**NickBolas90** continues to validate the alternative lands engine. The August 26 League 5-0 and August 27 Challenge list both retain [[Pawpatch Formation]], while [[Shifting Woodland]] remains part of the package. The important point is no longer that these cards can work once: Nick has now produced repeated published results with the same underlying plan across multiple weeks.

The August 29 Modern Challenge contains a sixth-place deck that MTGGoldfish labels "Yawgmoth," but the actual list is **Samwise Combo**: 4 [[Samwise Gamgee]], 4 [[Cauldron Familiar]], 4 [[Birthing Ritual]], 4 Chord, and only 1 Yawgmoth. It should not be counted as evidence for classic Golgari Yawgmoth.

## How Yawgmoth adapted this week

The most stable conventional configuration is now the **three-Yawgmoth, two-Chord, one-Insight shell**. Adam Kropidłowski's paper second place matches the structure The_Yoda used for two strong Challenge finishes in the preceding weeks. This branch keeps the Cauldron/GSZ core but trades some raw engine redundancy for instant-speed access to the exact creature needed.

The other durable branch is NickBolas90's **lands engine**. [[Mole Man, Moloid Master]], [[Icetill Explorer]], [[Ghost Quarter]], and [[Shifting Woodland]] convert the mana base into a recursive value package. The continued appearance of two Shifting Woodlands in recent versions makes it increasingly clear that Woodland is an engine card in this build rather than a novelty singleton.

[[Dredger's Insight]] remains unsettled. The Chord shell is comfortable at one copy, while Nick's lands build has generally wanted more. The zero-Insight Bowmasters/Grist configuration from mid-August has not produced another prominent published result, so there is not enough evidence to treat zero as the new default.

[[Endurance]] remains one of the best maindeck flex cards. Living End is still one of Yawgmoth's worst measured matchups, Esper Reanimator remains the largest archetype in the broad metagame, and Endurance is useful enough elsewhere that the game-one slot has a low opportunity cost.

## Decks pressuring Yawgmoth

**Izzet Prowess** remains the highest-priority common bad matchup. Yawgmoth is at roughly 40% over the current 30-day sample, and Prowess made up 22% of the August 28 Modern Challenge. That event's entire Top 4 was Izzet Prowess. Three Fatal Push is still the configuration I would prefer for an open field.

**Eldrazi Bloodchief Combo** remains another important unfavorable matchup, with Yawgmoth around 41% in the recent data. It was also 16% of the August 27 and August 28 Challenges. Cheap interaction and Thoughtseize matter more than slow grind cards here.

**Amulet Titan** is around 45% for Yawgmoth in the current sample. The matchup makes [[Ashiok, Dream Render]], [[Fulminator Mage]], and Thoughtseize particularly attractive because those cards also overlap with other decks.

**Living End** remains the ugliest recurring matchup: the current 30-day sample has Yawgmoth at 20%, albeit over only five matches. The exact percentage is noisy, but several consecutive weeks of poor results make the direction convincing. Keep the maindeck Endurance and additional graveyard interaction after sideboarding.

**Esper Blink** remains unfavorable at about 38% in the current sample and continues to perform well in events. The key is resource discipline: do not expose every engine piece to [[Wrath of the Skies]], and expect repeated exile removal rather than a single one-for-one exchange.

**Izzet Metalcraft** is now a very different story. Yawgmoth is at 60% over 20 recent matches. Metalcraft is still common enough that 2 Force of Vigor remains correct, but there is no current reason to devote extra sideboard space to fixing the matchup.

**Ruby Storm** is currently favorable in the available data, while Boros Energy and Esper Reanimator are close to even. These remain important practice matchups, but they are not where Yawgmoth's sideboard needs the most repair.

## Sideboard cards overperforming

[[Thoughtseize]] remains the most reliable three-of across successful Yawgmoth configurations. It also continues to rank among the most-played cards in Modern Challenges, reflecting how much of the current field is combo, graveyard, or stack-oriented.

[[Fatal Push]] is the card whose third copy has gained the most value this week. Izzet Prowess dominated the August 28 Challenge, and Eldrazi Bloodchief Combo remains both common and unfavorable. Adam Kropidłowski's sideboard uses all three.

[[Force of Vigor]] remains a stable two-of. Izzet Metalcraft is no longer statistically unfavorable, but artifact decks remain common enough that trimming the free interaction is unnecessary.

[[Endurance]] remains the best graveyard card to place in the main deck. Its value is reinforced by Living End's poor matchup numbers and Esper Reanimator's position near the top of the metagame.

[[Ashiok, Dream Render]] is the most interesting established sideboard card gaining ground. Adam used two copies, and NickBolas90 has also used the card in the lands build. It covers Amulet Titan, search-heavy combo, and graveyard strategies while providing a very different axis from creature removal.

[[Pawpatch Formation]] has now survived a second week in NickBolas90's successful build. Its value is flexibility: it answers a flying creature or an enchantment at instant speed, but can replace itself and make a Food when neither removal mode is relevant.

The broader metagame is heavily prepared for stack fights. [[Consign to Memory]] and [[Mystical Dispute]] were among the most-played cards in the August 27 and August 29 Challenges, while [[Vexing Bauble]] repeatedly ranked near the top. [[Surgical Extraction]] also surged in the August 28 and August 29 Challenge data. Blue decks are therefore likely to present unusually dense cheap interaction after sideboarding.

## New or newly relevant cards in Yawgmoth lists

There is no new breakout card this week.

[[Pawpatch Formation]] is the one emerging card whose case improved. It appeared in NickBolas90's August 26 League 5-0 and again in the August 27 Challenge list, following its first successful appearance the previous week. That is enough repeated evidence to treat it as a legitimate sideboard option rather than a one-off experiment.

[[Ashiok, Dream Render]] is not new, but two copies in Adam Kropidłowski's second-place list reinforce its current positioning. With Amulet Titan, Living End, Reanimator, and search-based combo all present, its overlap is unusually useful.

[[Chord of Calling]] likewise continues to gain evidence as a structural choice. The successful conventional lists no longer point cleanly toward the four-Yawgmoth, no-Chord shell that dominated earlier in the summer; the 3-Yawgmoth / 2-Chord configuration has now persisted for several weeks.
