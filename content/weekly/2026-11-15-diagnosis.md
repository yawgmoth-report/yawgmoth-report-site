**Classic Golgari Yawgmoth had no verified major-event breakthrough this week.** Most of the new 5-0 results labeled "Yawgmoth" are actually Samwise Gamgee/Birthing Ritual decks, and the latest matchup data for the classic Cauldron/Ballista shell is poor. The interesting genuine Yawgmoth card experiment is a singleton [[Darklight Phoenix]] in a November 10 six-player event. It warrants investigation, not a claim of an established new build.

MTGDecks currently reports a **37% trailing 30-day win rate** for its specifically classified Golgari Yawgmoth archetype, compared with approximately 43% in the previous weekly snapshot. This is a changing selected match sample, not a controlled week-over-week experiment. The clearest unfavorable pairings are Esper Blink, Izzet Affinity, Eldrazi Bloodchief Combo, and Izzet Prowess.

## Important classification correction

Four highly visible recent results **do not belong to classic Yawgmoth**:

- **Xerk, November 8 Challenge, second place (6-3):** four Samwise Gamgee, four Birthing Ritual, four Cauldron Familiar, one Yawgmoth.
- **AwesomPossum, November 9 League 5-0:** four Samwise, four Birthing Ritual, four Cauldron Familiar, one Yawgmoth.
- **theMutz, November 10 League 5-0:** four Samwise, four Birthing Ritual, four Cauldron Familiar, zero Yawgmoth.
- **Wiltron92, November 11 League 5-0:** four Samwise, four Birthing Ritual, four Cauldron Familiar, zero Yawgmoth.

These share some colors, lands, and sideboard cards but use a different primary engine. Their results do not establish the effectiveness of classic Yawgmoth or its maindeck changes.

**Bloodchief has the largest convincing unfavorable sample**, and the terrible Blink/Affinity results are directionally concerning even with modest match counts. Avoid treating 13% or 19% as precise long-run probabilities. The whole matchup picture is more informative than any one small sample.

MetaMage's October 17–November 15 Modern window puts **Broodscale first at 13.0% of matches and a 54.9% overall win rate**, followed by Rakdos Midrange (10.7% share), Prowess (9.1%), Azorius Control (7.7%), Boros Energy (6.3%), and Affinity (5.3%). These are broader-format data and should not be directly substituted for the specific Yawgmoth matchup table. In the November 14 Modern Challenge 16, Affinity won and took three of the Top 6 places, another reason not to shave artifact interaction.

## Sideboard priorities

The best-supported *practical* interaction core remains:

- **2 [[Force of Vigor]]** plus access to [[Outland Liberator]] or flexible permanent removal against Affinity and Broodscale's artifact components. The November 14 Challenge strongly reinforces artifact coverage.
- **3 [[Fatal Push]]** against creature-combo decks and Prowess. Both Athenani and adumb played three sideboard copies.
- **2–3 [[Thoughtseize]]** for broad combo and control coverage. It is one of the few cards that can interact with otherwise disparate plans before they are assembled.
- **1–2 [[Ashiok, Dream Render]]** where Amulet/search strategies warrant it. Both recent reference lists use the card.
- **1–2 [[Abrupt Decay]]** as flexible permanent interaction, particularly if expecting a broad local field.

[[Endurance]], [[Vexing Bauble]], and [[Culling Ritual]] remain useful flexible slots, but none should be promoted on the basis of unsupported card-level win-rate claims. These judgments come from lists, overlap with current enemies, and matchup structure, **not** measured individual sideboard impact.

There is no reason to dedicate additional sideboard slots specifically to [[Grafdigger's Cage]] without new evidence of real harm from current opposing builds.

## New card watch: Darklight Phoenix

[[Darklight Phoenix]] debuted in *Reality Fracture* on October 23, 2026. It is a four-mana 3/2 flying, haste creature that returns from the graveyard at the beginning of combat on your turn if two or more creatures died earlier that turn. Yawgmoth's sacrifice ability can naturally meet the condition; in principle, Phoenix provides repeatable airborne pressure after a sacrifice-heavy main phase.

However, the card appears only as a **one-of in adumb's 2-1 six-player event** among the genuine recent lists verified in this pass. It is not green and cannot be found by Green Sun's Zenith. It also remains vulnerable to exile and graveyard interaction. This is a low-confidence experiment, not an independently established upgrade or a reason to reconstruct the deck.

## Diagnosis and next test

The evidence this week argues for **holding the conventional core steady and testing early interaction**, not adding a speculative engine. Compare Athenani's recursive-lands setup, Moorehardcore's four-Yawgmoth Rumble/Bowmasters setup, and—only as an optional curiosity—the one-Phoenix conventional list. Give the most practice time to Eldrazi Bloodchief/Broodscale, Affinity, Esper Blink, and Prowess. Track whether Fatal Push, Force, and flexible permanent interaction actually improve game one and post-board play before changing the established main engine.
