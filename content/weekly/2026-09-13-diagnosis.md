Yawgmoth remains healthy, but this week's successful lists argue against treating any one recent experimental branch as the new stock build. **NukaCola** went 5-0 in the September 9 MTGO League with a no-[[Chord of Calling]], no-lands-package list featuring 3 [[Orcish Bowmasters]] and 3 [[Dredger's Insight]]. **casper_x** and **TheMeatMan** both went 5-0 on September 7, with casper_x continuing to use the [[Mole Man, Moloid Master]] land-recursion package. In paper, **Zane Toledo** went 5-2-1 in the 132-player September 11 SCG CON Baltimore $1K RCQ.

A major Dallas result that became fully indexed after last week's report also deserves attention: **Shelkofsky finished 9th at 12-3 in the 921-player Magic Spotlight: The Hobbit at SCG CON Dallas**. That list is the strongest recent tournament result for classic Golgari Yawgmoth and introduces several unusual one-ofs: maindeck [[Spore Frog]] and [[Bridgeworks Battle]], plus [[Burning-Tree Shaman]] and [[Run Afoul]] in the sideboard.

The broader archetype remains solid. MTGDecks currently reports a 54% 30-day win rate. The current problem matchups are concentrated around Esper Blink, Living End, Amulet Titan, Hollow One, and Devoted Druid Combo, while Boros Energy and Izzet Prowess have both improved substantially in the latest sample.

## Notes on the lists

**Shelkofsky** is the most important benchmark. The 12-3 Spotlight list plays 3 [[Yawgmoth, Thran Physician]], 3 [[Walking Ballista]], 4 [[Agatha's Soul Cauldron]], 3 [[Green Sun's Zenith]], 3 [[Malevolent Rumble]], 3 [[Dredger's Insight]], and only 1 [[Chord of Calling]]. The creature package includes the normal 4 [[Badgermole Cub]], 4 [[Delighted Halfling]], 4 [[Young Wolf]], plus singleton [[Endurance]], [[Formidable Speaker]], [[Ouroboroid]], [[Virulent Emissary]], and the unusual [[Spore Frog]].

The other novel maindeck slot is [[Bridgeworks Battle]] // Tanglespan Bridgeworks. It functions as a green land when needed and as creature interaction when flooded, giving the deck a removal spell without paying the full opportunity cost of a spell slot.

Shelkofsky's sideboard is 3 [[Fatal Push]], 2 [[Force of Vigor]], 2 [[Thoughtseize]], and one each of [[Burning-Tree Shaman]], [[Damping Sphere]], Endurance, [[Grist, the Hunger Tide]], [[Kraul Harpooner]], [[Outland Liberator]], [[Run Afoul]], and [[Soulless Jailer]]. It notably contains no [[Vexing Bauble]], [[Ashiok, Dream Render]], or [[Culling Ritual]].

**NukaCola** gives a very different successful configuration. The September 9 5-0 plays 3 Yawgmoth, 3 Ballista, 4 Cauldron, 3 Malevolent Rumble, 3 Dredger's Insight, only 2 Green Sun's Zenith, and 3 [[Orcish Bowmasters]]. There is no Chord and no Mole Man/Icetill Explorer/Shifting Woodland package.

NukaCola's sideboard returns to a more conventional structure: 3 Fatal Push, 2 Force of Vigor, 2 Thoughtseize, then singleton [[Ashiok, Dream Render]], [[Culling Ritual]], Endurance, Grist, Outland Liberator, Soulless Jailer, and Vexing Bauble.

**casper_x** shows that the lands package is still viable. The September 7 5-0 includes [[Mole Man, Moloid Master]], so the package did not disappear after last week's results. The evidence now supports multiple legitimate branches rather than a single solved configuration.

The September 11 Baltimore paper lists continue to favor creature-based utility. [[Outland Liberator]] appears in successful Yawgmoth lists from Zane Toledo, Josh James, and Pbuhl11, making it one of the strongest repeated sideboard/tutor signals of the week.

## How Yawgmoth adapted this week

The main conclusion is **divergence rather than convergence**. The lands engine remains successful, but it is not taking over the archetype. NukaCola's 5-0 succeeds without Mole Man, Icetill Explorer, Shifting Woodland, or Chord. Shelkofsky's much larger-event result uses only one Chord and no lands package. The safest current core remains Cauldron, Yawgmoth, Ballista, Badgermole Cub, Young Wolf, Halfling, Rumble, Insight, and GSZ; the final engine and utility slots are still open.

[[Dredger's Insight]] has strengthened its position again. Shelkofsky and NukaCola both use three copies, and the card remains present across recent 5-0 lists. The zero-Insight Bowmasters/Grist experiment from August has not become the default.

[[Chord of Calling]] looks best as a small toolbox supplement rather than a mandatory multi-copy engine. Shelkofsky's 12-3 list plays exactly one. The two-Chord configurations remain credible, especially in paper, but recent online results show that Chord is not required.

[[Orcish Bowmasters]] remains similarly unsettled. NukaCola's three-copy 5-0 is strong evidence that the card can be a real package, but aggregate recent Yawgmoth data still shows it as a minority flex card rather than a universal inclusion.

## Decks pressuring Yawgmoth

**Esper Blink** is the clearest current problem among major decks. Yawgmoth is at 36% over 11 recent matches. The matchup attacks on exactly the axis Yawgmoth dislikes: repeated exile removal, sweepers, and a long game that remains powerful after the first engine is answered.

**Living End** remains poor at 33% over six recent matches. The sample is small but the direction has persisted for several weeks. A maindeck Endurance plus additional graveyard interaction remains justified.

**Amulet Titan** is at 44% over nine matches. The sample is again modest, but it remains one of the most consistently troublesome established archetypes. Thoughtseize, Ashiok, land interaction, and a fast clock remain the cleanest plan.

**Hollow One** is 40% over five matches. That is too small a sample for precise conclusions, but it belongs on the practice list because its speed and recursive threats can punish slow engine hands.

**Devoted Druid Combo** is 46% over 13 matches. The matchup has become more relevant as Devoted Druid remains present in current event results. Cheap interaction matters more than slow value pieces.

The largest change from last week is that **Izzet Prowess and Boros Energy are no longer showing up as bad matchups**. Yawgmoth is currently 54% over 24 matches against Prowess and 65% over 17 against Boros Energy. Eldrazi Bloodchief Combo is also close to even at 52% over 25, while Esper Reanimator is 52% over 33.

## Sideboard cards overperforming

The strongest common package across the best recent lists is **3 [[Fatal Push]], 2 [[Force of Vigor]], and 2 [[Thoughtseize]]**. Both Shelkofsky's 12-3 list and NukaCola's 5-0 use exactly those quantities.

[[Outland Liberator]] is the strongest utility-creature signal. It appears in Shelkofsky's Spotlight sideboard, NukaCola's League sideboard, and multiple September 11 Baltimore Yawgmoth lists. The combination of artifact/enchantment interaction and tutorability continues to justify the slot.

[[Soulless Jailer]] also appears in both Shelkofsky and NukaCola. Living End remains a bad matchup, Esper Reanimator remains one of the most-played decks, and Jailer attacks graveyard strategies on a creature body that fits Yawgmoth's tutor infrastructure.

[[Endurance]] remains similarly robust: one main is still common, with a second copy available after sideboarding in strong recent configurations.

The two genuinely unusual successful sideboard cards are [[Burning-Tree Shaman]] and [[Run Afoul]] from Shelkofsky's 12-3. Burning-Tree Shaman punishes repeated nonmana activated abilities, while Run Afoul is a one-mana answer to flying creatures that does not target the creature itself.

Opposing decks continue to devote substantial space to cheap interaction. [[Consign to Memory]], [[Vexing Bauble]], [[Spell Snare]], [[Dismember]], and [[Mystical Dispute]] were among the most-played cards in the September 9 published League set. Yawgmoth should expect post-board games where resolving a single expensive engine spell is difficult.

## New or newly relevant cards in Yawgmoth lists

[[Spore Frog]] is the clearest new maindeck creature. It is a one-mana green creature that can sacrifice itself to prevent combat damage for a turn, making it accessible to Green Sun's Zenith and Chord. A single 12-3 result is not enough to make it stock, but the result is large enough that the card deserves testing.

[[Bridgeworks Battle]] is the other meaningful maindeck innovation. Its modal land face lowers the deckbuilding cost substantially: it can occupy a mana-source slot while still becoming removal in games where the land is unnecessary.

[[Burning-Tree Shaman]] is a new sideboard bullet that required Shelkofsky to include a [[Stomping Ground]]. It is therefore the highest-cost experiment structurally, but the 12-3 result makes the choice worth understanding.

[[Run Afoul]] is a narrow but extremely efficient one-mana sideboard card against flyers. Its sacrifice wording gives it a different role from ordinary targeted removal.

The important point is that all four cards come from the strongest recent large-event Yawgmoth performance, but none has yet repeated in another successful list. They should be treated as high-value tests rather than new stock cards.
