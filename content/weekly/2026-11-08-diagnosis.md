**The best new classic Yawgmoth result is Athenani's November 3 MTGO League 5-0, achieved with the Mole Man/Icetill Explorer land-recursion package.** Moorehardcore also finished 4-2, twelfth of 54 players, in the November 1 Modern Challenge. These results keep two distinct configurations credible, but the overall archetype continues to struggle in a field dominated by Broodscale and supplemented by Prowess, control, and Affinity.

The November 3 list is noteworthy precisely because it differs from Moorehardcore's late-October trophies. Athenani plays **3 Yawgmoth, 3 Ballista, 3 Green Sun's Zenith, 3 Malevolent Rumble, 3 Dredger's Insight**, and one each of **Mole Man, Moloid Master; Icetill Explorer; Ghost Quarter; and Shifting Woodland**, with no Orcish Bowmasters or Chord. Moorehardcore stays on **4 Yawgmoth, 2 Bowmasters, 4 Rumble, 3 Insight, 2 GSZ**, with no recursive-lands engine.

**No genuinely new card has established itself in classic Yawgmoth this week.** The lands package has returned in a trophy list but remains a low-adoption option rather than an established default. There is no purchase signal strong enough to justify a new recommendation.

## What changed this week

**Athenani has revived the recursive-lands branch.** The complete main deck has four Badgermole Cub, four Delighted Halfling, four Young Wolf, three Walking Ballista, three Yawgmoth, one each of Dryad Arbor, Endurance, Formidable Speaker, Icetill Explorer, Mole Man, Ouroboroid, and Strangleroot Geist, and one Grist. Its noncreature core is four Agatha's Soul Cauldron, three Green Sun's Zenith, three Malevolent Rumble, and three Dredger's Insight. Its twenty-one lands include a singleton Ghost Quarter and Shifting Woodland.

The sideboard is **3 Fatal Push, 3 Thoughtseize, 2 Force of Vigor, 2 Ashiok, Dream Render, 2 Vexing Bauble**, plus one each of Abrupt Decay, Outland Liberator, and a second Ghost Quarter. This is an especially clean board for comparing land interaction with conventional disruptive slots.

**Moorehardcore continues the no-lands-engine branch.** Four Yawgmoths and four Rumbles maximize access to the namesake card and selection, while two Bowmasters provide early interaction. The November 1 finish does not imply that this build is superior to Athenani's, nor that it solves Yawgmoth's bad matchups.

These choices should be viewed against their adoption rates. In the October 9–November 7 published-list sample, Dredger's Insight appears in **98%** of classic Yawgmoth lists (three copies in 86%, a fourth in 19%); a fourth Yawgmoth appears in only **10%**; Bowmasters appears in **64%**; and Chord of Calling in **26%**. Mole Man and Icetill Explorer are still rare. The latest successful lists thus point to **multiple viable flex configurations**, not convergence on a single newly discovered shell.

## The decks actually pressuring Yawgmoth

The current MTGDecks trailing 30-day matchup table puts Yawgmoth around **43% overall**. Its better-sampled problems are:

| Opponent | Yawgmoth win rate | Matches | Practical emphasis |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Eldrazi Bloodchief Combo | **32%** | **59** | Removal, hand disruption, artifact interaction |
| Izzet Prowess | **31%** | **32** | Cheap removal, early survival |
| Izzet Affinity | **27%** | **26** | Force of Vigor, flexible artifact removal |
| Esper Blink | **27%** | **26** | Play around Solitude and sweepers; preserve a rebuild |
| Azorius Control | **43%** | **14** | Pressure while retaining access to engines |
| Amulet Titan | **22%** | **9** | Ashiok, discard, land interaction; small sample |
| Ruby Storm | **20%** | **10** | Thoughtseize, Bauble and broad disruption; small sample |
| Living End | **33%** | **6** | Maintain graveyard coverage; very small sample |

These percentages are **Yawgmoth's wins**, not the opponent's. The small samples for Titan, Storm, and Living End should not be interpreted as precise matchup estimates. Conversely, Bloodchief, Prowess, Affinity, and Esper Blink have enough observations to justify real preparation.

The broader Modern field reinforces this assessment. MetaMage's October 10–November 8 window identifies **Broodscale as the most-played deck at 16.2% of matches (54.1% overall win rate)**. Prowess follows at 9.5%, Azorius Control at 7.5%, Boros Energy at 5.8%, and Affinity at 5.4%. The Broodscale share is a metagame measurement, **not** a separately verified current Yawgmoth-versus-Broodscale win percentage. Avoid conflating Broodscale and Bloodchief classifications across data sources.

### Matchup approach

**Broodscale and Eldrazi Bloodchief:** Prioritize stopping an active Basking Broodscale/Blade of the Bloodchief line with instant-speed interaction, then use artifact disruption against support cards. Ghost Quarter can be valuable against Urza's Saga or an Eldrazi mana base, but sorcery-speed land disruption is not a substitute for direct combo answers.

**Prowess:** The 31% matchup over 32 matches is a strong reason to keep three Fatal Push. Arasta of the Endless Web remains a possible single-copy experiment, not a demonstrated default: four mana or a five-mana Green Sun's Zenith is often too slow on the draw.

**Affinity:** Keep both Force of Vigor and consider the tutorable Outland Liberator. Flexible answers that remain useful against other artifacts are preferable to a narrow sideboard dedicated exclusively to this opponent.

**Esper Blink and Azorius Control:** Protect the ability to rebuild after exile removal and sweepers. A more concentrated combo plan can be punished by Solitude; the critical tests are postboard sequencing and hand selection, not simply faster goldfish draws.

**Amulet and Ruby Storm:** Their percentages are unstable because of small samples, but Thoughtseize, Ashiok, and Vexing Bauble overlap well enough with other decks to preserve coverage.

## Sideboard cards with the strongest evidence

A representative open-field board is now close to **2 Force of Vigor, 3 Fatal Push, 2–3 Thoughtseize, 1–2 Ashiok, 1–2 Vexing Bauble**, with the remaining slots chosen for overlap. This is consistent with Athenani's successful November 3 configuration and Moorehardcore's recent lists.

In the October 9–November 7 classic-Yawgmoth published-list sample, the relevant adoption figures are approximately **100% Force of Vigor, 97% Fatal Push, 88% Endurance, 86% Thoughtseize, 84% Ashiok, 79% Abrupt Decay, 74% Vexing Bauble, 64% Culling Ritual, and 59% Soulless Jailer**. Adoption is not a direct estimate of each card's sideboard win-rate contribution; it is evidence of which options have repeatedly appeared in published configurations.

**Force of Vigor** remains the easiest inclusion to defend because of Broodscale and Affinity. **Fatal Push** offers the best broad improvement against creature combo and fast red decks. **Ashiok** has overlapping applications against search-based strategies. **Thoughtseize** remains effective as a flexible answer to spell-combo and control decks; **Vexing Bauble** continues to cover free-spell interaction and various combo lines. **Outland Liberator** is a flexible Green Sun's Zenith target rather than dedicated hate for any single opposing sideboard card.

Avoid disproportionately dedicating sideboard space to Grafdigger's Cage. There is no new evidence this week that it should drive Yawgmoth's plans. More urgent problems are the opposing deck's primary combo, early pressure, sweepers, and broadly applicable artifact interaction.

## Newly appearing cards and priorities

There is **no verified new breakthrough card for classic Yawgmoth during November 1–8**. The conspicuous change is the return of the **Mole Man / Icetill Explorer / Ghost Quarter / Shifting Woodland** package in Athenani's 5-0. It is an older, low-adoption package and should be tested as an alternative to the conventional Rumble/Bowmasters build, not treated as a newly established staple.

Similarly, a fourth Yawgmoth has succeeded in Moorehardcore's recent configuration, but the broader adoption figures still favor three. Neither that variation nor this week's sideboard choices justify a new acquisition on their own.

**Testing recommendation:** Start with Athenani's exact 75 and Moorehardcore's four-Yawgmoth configuration. Compare them across Broodscale/Bloodchief, Prowess, Affinity, and Esper Blink. Separate games with and without sideboarding and note whether Ghost Quarter plus recursion generates enough value to compensate for the absence of Bowmasters. Both configurations rely primarily on existing established Yawgmoth cards.
