This week produced **two genuinely different Yawgmoth 5-0 decks**. TheMeatMan's November 19 MTGO League trophy is classic Golgari Cauldron/Ballista Yawgmoth with **two [[Dr. Leonard "Bones" McCoy]]**, the first worthwhile new-card experiment in the familiar shell for several weeks. Gartxot's November 22 trophy instead plays **four [[Yawgmoth, Thran Physician]], four [[Birthing Ritual]], four [[Salt Vampire]], and four [[Orcish Bowmasters]]**, with no Agatha's Soul Cauldron, Walking Ballista, Green Sun's Zenith or Dredger's Insight. This second list deserves attention as a distinct Yawgmoth/Ritual branch, **not** as evidence that classic Cauldron Yawgmoth now wants four Salt Vampires.

There was also a paper Top 8 by Nakagawa Shota in a 19-player November 21 Chiba qualifier. That deck is more conventional, combining Cauldron/Ballista with two Chord of Calling and singletons [[Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER]] and [[Virulent Emissary]]. These results show players trying at least three approaches, but the larger published-list sample remains very poor for classic Yawgmoth: DeckSnipe's October 24–November 22 window shows **9 wins, 21 losses, one draw** across 31 reported tournament matches, approximately 30% wins excluding the draw. This is a small, selective sample, not proof that the underlying matchup win rate is 30%; League 5-0 finishes do not enter that match-level calculation.

## Recent successful Yawgmoth lists

### November 22 — Birthing Ritual Yawgmoth, not conventional Cauldron Yawgmoth

Gartxot plays four Yawgmoths, four Young Wolves, four Badgermole Cubs, four Bowmasters, four new Salt Vampires, three Chord of Calling, four Birthing Ritual, and only two Malevolent Rumble. [[Azog, Moria's Ruin]] and [[Boggart Trawler]] are further departures. There is no Cauldron/Ballista loop. The sideboard uses three Force of Vigor, three Fatal Push, two Ashiok, two Vexing Bauble, and one each of Abrupt Decay, Endurance, Thoughtseize, Soulless Jailer, and [[Chomping Changeling]].

This is a sacrifice-and-creature-value build centered on Yawgmoth and Ritual rather than the familiar Cauldron package. It should be tested and evaluated separately from classic Golgari Yawgmoth and from the Samwise Gamgee/Cauldron Familiar/Birthing Ritual decks that aggregators sometimes place under the same broad label.

### November 19 — the new classic Yawgmoth experiment

{{< deck-card title="TheMeatMan" variant="Cauldron / Ballista with two Dr. McCoy" event="MTGO Modern League #11337" date="November 19" result="5-0" url="https://mtgpile.com/deck/modern-league-2026-11-1911337-themeatman" source="MTGO / MTGpile" notes="2 Dr. Leonard Bones McCoy; 3 Yawgmoth, 3 Ballista, 4 Cauldron, 3 Zenith, 3 Rumble, 3 Insight" >}}

TheMeatMan's 60 is otherwise a conventional Cauldron shell: three Yawgmoths, three Ballistas, four Cauldrons, three Zenith, three Rumble, three Insight, four Cubs, four Halflings, four Young Wolves, and utility creatures. There is no Chord of Calling, Bowmasters, recursive-lands package, or blue splash. Two [[Dr. Leonard "Bones" McCoy]] and a maindeck [[Haywire Mite]] distinguish the list. The 15-card sideboard is especially coherent: three Fatal Push; two each of Force of Vigor, Ashiok, Thoughtseize and Vexing Bauble; and one each of Abrupt Decay, Culling Ritual, Endurance and Soulless Jailer.

## Why the two new cards matter

**[[Dr. Leonard "Bones" McCoy]]**, from November's *Star Trek* set, costs {1}{G}{G}, has flash, and is a 3/2 green creature. Its enter-the-battlefield ability can return a non-Doctor permanent from your graveyard to hand, or choose another creature so that if it dies this turn, it comes back at the next end step with a +1/+1 counter. It can recover Cauldron, Yawgmoth, Ballista, or a land; it can also provide a reactive extra life to a key creature. Unlike some other recursion effects, it is Zenith-accessible, although tutoring for it is not itself an instant-speed play. A crucial limitation is that returning a Young Wolf or Strangleroot Geist with a +1/+1 counter interferes with subsequent undying triggers. Two copies in a single League-winning list are promising, but not yet independent confirmation or proof that the card fixes the Esper Blink matchup.

**[[Salt Vampire]]**, also from *Star Trek*, is a {1}{B} 2/2 lifelinker. Its enter-the-battlefield ability reveals an opponent's hand to you and optionally exiles one nonland card, after which that player draws a replacement. It offers selective disruption, information, and some life gain without producing outright card advantage. The replacement draw is a real cost: sometimes it finds another threat. Four copies make sense as a deliberate plan in a Birthing Ritual creature shell, especially alongside a full set of Bowmasters. But that is very different from establishing it as a four-of in Cauldron Yawgmoth.

**[[Chomping Changeling]]** is a smaller sideboard observation. It is a {2}{G} creature with changeling and an enter-the-battlefield artifact/enchantment removal ability. Gartxot's singleton offers an answer that Chord and Birthing Ritual can find, but its role overlaps cards already common in Golgari such as Haywire Mite and Outland Liberator. It is not a major new acquisition priority.

## Metagame and bad matchups

MetaMage's October 24–November 22 sample contains 6,226 matches. The most-played deck is **Broodscale, 13.6% of matches**, with a **54.7% overall win rate across 844 matches**. Prowess accounts for about 10.0% of matches, Rakdos Midrange 9.9%, Azorius Control 7.8%, Boros Energy 6.7%, Affinity 5.1%, Esper Blink 3.8%, and Amulet Titan 3.8%. Amulet is doing particularly well in the aggregate at **57.4% over 236 matches**. These are whole-format figures, **not** Yawgmoth-specific matchup percentages.

The accessible MTGDecks Yawgmoth matchup table remains bleak; its trailing-match samples should not be confused with the one-week window of this report:

| Opponent | Yawgmoth win rate in displayed sample | Matches | Practical priority |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Eldrazi Bloodchief Combo | 30% | 46 | Kill the creature combo early; use discard and artifact interaction |
| Izzet Affinity | 19% | 16 | Force of Vigor; efficient permanent answers |
| Esper Blink | 13% | 15 | Preserve rebuild capacity against exile removal and sweepers |
| Izzet Prowess | 23% | 13 | Cheap removal, early blockers, sensible mulligans |
| Amulet Titan | 25% | 8 | Ashiok and discard; small sample |
| Ruby Storm | 22% | 9 | Thoughtseize and spell interaction; small sample |

These percentages are noisy, especially for 8–16 matches, and should not be extrapolated as exact future probabilities. The broader pattern, however, continues to be unfavorable against Blink, Affinity, Prowess and creature combo. Broodscale's sheer presence makes creature/artifact interaction especially important. The new cards offer hypotheses to test, not evidence that these matchups have already been repaired.

## Sideboard cards earning space

The most consistent sideboard package in recent classic Yawgmoth is **three Fatal Push, two Force of Vigor, two Ashiok, two Thoughtseize, two Vexing Bauble**, plus flexible one-ofs. TheMeatMan uses precisely this structure. The small 11-list DeckSnipe October 24–November 22 published-list sample shows Fatal Push, Force of Vigor, Ashiok, Abrupt Decay and Vexing Bauble in all 11 lists; Endurance, Soulless Jailer and Thoughtseize in 10 of 11; and Culling Ritual in nine. These are adoption rates among published lists, **not** direct proof of increased match win rates attributable to any one card.

**Force of Vigor** looks especially important when Broodscale and Affinity are prevalent; the November 22 Birthing Ritual build uses three instead of the two in the classic November 19 list. **Fatal Push** remains the best-supported cheap answer to Prowess and creature-based combo. **Ashiok** gains value when Amulet Titan is successful, and **Thoughtseize** gives broad interaction with combo and control. The flex slot decision should follow the expected local field: Culling Ritual for wide/low-cost permanent boards, Endurance for graveyard decks, and removal creatures for artifact/enchantment engines. Be mindful of the tension between your own Vexing Bauble and pitching Force of Vigor without paying mana.

Grafdigger's Cage is not a principal sideboard driver here. No dedicated Cage-answer package is justified by the findings this week; flexible answers should be chosen for the decks and permanents that actually matter.

## Conclusion and next test

**TheMeatMan's two-McCoy classic shell is the first experiment to run.** It stays close to a conventional Yawgmoth list and therefore permits a relatively clean test: compare its two McCoy slots against the conventional Bowmasters/utility-creature slots while preserving the remaining engine. Observe whether McCoy meaningfully improves rebuilding after removal, and whether the card remains useful when graveyards are pressured.

**Gartxot's Salt Vampire build is a separate, higher-variance experiment.** It is a real 5-0 with four Yawgmoths but removes the defining Cauldron/Ballista finish. Evaluate its matchups on its own terms, and do not merge its card choices uncritically into classic Yawgmoth. Two leagues and one small paper Top 8 are encouraging developments, not sufficient evidence to declare an archetype-level turnaround.
